Departure — Balingasa, Quezon City

Midnight departure from 106 Metom St., Commonwealth, Quezon City. Enter NLEX via the Mindanao Avenue Toll Gate. Driving at midnight beats the daytime heat and heavy traffic, giving you a smooth, cooler ride all the way north.

💡 Tips Prepare snacks and drinks beforehand. Assign a co-driver for the NLEX stretch. Bring exact change or an e-pass (Easytrip/Autosweep) for the toll. Make sure the car is fueled up before departure.

Manaoag Church — Quick Stopover

A beloved pilgrimage site in Pangasinan — the Minor Basilica of Our Lady of the Rosary of Manaoag. The church opens at 4:00 AM.

💡 Tips Dress modestly (cover shoulders and knees). Parking is street-side — allow a few minutes to park. The church area is serene and cool at this hour. Use the restroom here before continuing.

Back on the Road — Heading North

After Manaoag, continue north along the MacArthur Highway (Pan-Philippine Highway) toward La Union. The drive from Manaoag to Agoo is approximately 1.5 hours. The morning light over the Pangasinan plains at this hour is stunning.

💡 Tips Tune into a good playlist or podcast for the drive. The road passes through Urdaneta and San Carlos — fuel stops are available if needed. The sunrise will be out around 5:30 AM — enjoy the golden hour on the highway!

Agoo Eco Park Beach

Your first taste of La Union's coastline! Agoo Eco Park is a well-maintained beach park in Santa Rita, Agoo — perfect for a picnic breakfast while enjoying the sea breeze. The park features a walkable shoreline, relaxing greenery, and a laid-back atmosphere that's ideal for your first morning rest stop.

💡 Tips There is an entrance fee (separate receipts for different areas). Bring your own breakfast — pack sandwiches, pandesal, or instant noodles from a thermos for a beach picnic! Cottages are near the entrance. If you want to be closer to the sea, take a walk along the shore. Bring extra plastic bags for trash disposal.

The Mansion La Union — Coffee & Photos

The Mansion La Union sits atop a hill in Talogtog, San Juan, offering a breathtaking panoramic view of San Juan Beach and the South China Sea. Its hilltop coffee shop serves espresso-based drinks and light refreshments. It's one of the most Instagrammable spots in La Union, with a rustic-chic aesthetic perfect for group photos.

💡 Tips The road going up is very steep with 3 challenging sections — make sure your driver is confident on inclines! Low-clearance vehicles should take extra care. There's also a pedestrian stairway if some prefer to walk up. Come before 11 AM to beat the crowd. Order the iced latte — reviewers love it! Clear weather offers the best photo opportunities.

Auntie Ludy's House — Camansi, San Fernando

Arrive at your home base in Camansi, San Fernando, La Union. After the long drive since midnight, this is your most important rest stop of the day — lunch with family, a nap, and time to freshen up before the afternoon beach adventure. Nothing beats home-cooked Filipino food after a road trip!

💡 Tips Rest at least 2–3 hours before heading to the beach. Change into swimwear and pack a beach bag: sunscreen, towels, extra clothes, cash (for food and surf rental). San Fernando City is close to all the major beach towns of La Union.

Park & Surf → Urbiztondo & San Juan Beach

Urbiztondo Beach in San Juan, La Union is the surfing capital of the Philippines' north. Park at Park & Surf (₱200/car, accept GCash/cash) steps from the beach. Spend the golden afternoon hours swimming, trying surfing lessons (beginner boards available from local surf schools), and exploring the strip of cafés, restaurants, and surf shops along Urbiztondo's famous beachfront.

💡 Tips Surfing lessons typically cost ₱300–₱500/hour with board rental included. The waves are strong — non-swimmers should stay in shallow areas. Must-try eats along the strip: La Union Brewery, Flotsam & Jetsam, Maríwasa. The beach gets gorgeous at golden hour around 5–6 PM. Head back by 7 PM to avoid traffic leaving San Juan.

Return to Auntie Ludy's for the Night

Head back to Camansi, San Fernando for dinner and rest. It's been a long but incredible first day — from midnight in Quezon City to a Pangasinan church, a beach picnic, mountain coffee, and surfing in La Union. Sleep early; Day 2 starts at 5 AM!

Early Rise & Breakfast at Auntie Ludy's

Rise early for a home-cooked breakfast at Auntie Ludy's before a packed Day 2. Today takes you from La Union's coastline up into the mountains of Baguio — a stunning contrast of sea and pine. Departure from Auntie Ludy's House

Leave from Camansi Bridge, San Fernando, La Union. Head south along MacArthur Highway toward Bauang for the Gapuz Grapes Farm. The drive to Bauang takes roughly 30–40 minutes from San Fernando, passing through the agricultural lowlands of La Union.

💡 Tips Say your thank-yous and goodbyes properly! Leave the place as clean as you found it. The road from San Fernando to Bauang is well-paved and straightforward.

Gapuz Grapes Farm — Bauang, La Union

Gapuz Grapes Farm is one of the most unique agri-tourism stops in northern Luzon — a working vineyard in the lowlands of Bauang where you can actually pick grapes straight from the vine. May is peak grape season, making your timing ideal! The farm sells fresh grapes by the kilo (around ₱350/kg) and produces homemade grape wine, jam, and vinegar.

💡 Tips Call ahead to confirm availability: +63 917 554 0966. The road to the farm is bumpy — not recommended for lowered cars. Don't follow signs on the highway — follow Google Maps to the actual Gapuz Farm. Guides are available at the entrance. Stock up on grape wine and pastillas as pasalubong! Farm opens at 7 AM.

Tan-aw sa Alang — Lunch with a View

Tan-aw sa Alang on the Ben Palispis Highway in Tuba, Benguet is a hidden gem perched overlooking forested mountains and mini-terraces. The name literally means "view from the mountain gap" — and it delivers. Open all day for breakfast items, they're famous for their Tokwa and Bagnet, Benguet brewed coffee with balikutsa (native sugar candy), and freshly baked banana bread. On cool, foggy days, you literally dine inside the clouds.

💡 Tips Opens at 7 AM — they serve all-day breakfast. Must order: Tokwa & Bagnet (5★ reviewed), Brewed Benguet Coffee with balikutsa, Banana Bread. Pet-friendly! They also sell local pasalubong. Phone: +63 998 887 9216. They close at 6 PM weekdays, 7 PM weekends.

Meet Relatives — SM Baguio Meetup Point

Meeting point pinned at SM Baguio — the iconic mall perched on a cliff with stunning views. SM Baguio is easy to find, has ample parking, and makes an excellent meeting hub for the whole group. Spend time catching up with relatives before heading to Burnham Park.

⚠️ PMA Graduation Day Alert Avoid Loakan Road, Camp John Hay, and roads near the Philippine Military Academy today. Heavy traffic and road closures are expected. Use Marcos Highway or Kennon Road as alternatives.
💡 Tips SM Baguio has a rooftop park with panoramic city views — great for a quick photo. Starbucks here overlooks the mountains — iconic! Validate parking inside. The mall is a good spot to shop for ukay-ukay or Baguio ref magnets.

Burnham Park Picnic — Baguio City

Burnham Park is the green heart of Baguio City, a beloved public park named after American architect Daniel Burnham who designed Baguio's city plan. The park features a scenic artificial lake with boat rentals, a rose garden, a children's playground, and wide open lawns perfect for a late-afternoon picnic. The cool mountain air at this hour is divine.

💡 Tips Rent bikes around the park (₱30–₱60/30 min). Food stalls and kiosks sell Baguio favorites: strawberry taho, peanut brittle, and ube pastillas. The park is open 24 hours and free to enter! Leave by 5:30 PM latest to beat the Baguio exit traffic.

L'Atelier Du Grain — Pasalubong & Homeward

L'Atelier Du Grain on Military Cutoff Road is Baguio's most beloved artisan bakery — famous for impossibly buttery croissants, elegant tart shells, strawberry cheesecake tarts, and French-inspired pastries. One reviewer called it "a non-negotiable Baguio stop." Pick up boxes of pastries as the ultimate pasalubong before beginning the drive home to Manila. Opens at 8 AM, closes at 8 PM.

💡 Tips Minimum order ₱500 — they accept credit cards. Parking is tight; arrive early or have someone wait in the car. Must-buy: Croissants, Strawberry Cheesecake Tart, Tart Shells. The ground floor is compact — order quickly and enjoy pastries on the way home. Phone: +63 908 759 1878

Homeward Bound — Back to Quezon City

Begin the drive back to Metro Manila. From Baguio, take Marcos Highway (recommended — avoids PMA area) down to Pangasinan, then back through TPLEX and NLEX toward Quezon City. The drive is roughly 6–7 hours depending on traffic. Expect NLEX to be lighter late evening.

💡 Drive Home Tips Use Waze or Google Maps for live traffic updates. Take driver breaks every 2 hours. TPLEX rest areas have good pit-stop facilities. Avoid Kennon Road at night — it can be foggy and winding. Enjoy your croissants on the road!
